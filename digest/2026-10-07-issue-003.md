## 1. Protocols & standards

**The week's biggest item: the Personal Agent Protocol.** Sierra + Meta published a joint proposal Oct 6 at Meta Connect — an open standard for personal agents acting on behalf of consumers, OAuth-based, tiered access (guests check stock; signed-in users grant read or write; sessions carry across channels), with activity logs so businesses see what agents do on their sites. Founding partners: Walmart, Stripe, Shopify, Genesys, NiCE, Decagon, Instinct, Rocket. **v0.1 spec due late October**; design workshops + reference implementation after. Two load-bearing facts: OpenAI and Anthropic are not in the coalition, and Amazon has been actively *blocking* Meta's agents over scraping fears (plus sued Perplexity). The direct rival is Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol from June. This is the second agent-commerce standard courting the same acquirers in four months — and only 3% of US adults trust AI agents with purchases, per one analysis of the launch. Announcement-stage: spec not public, payments deferred to future extensions. ([unite.ai](https://www.unite.ai/sierra-unveils-personal-agent-protocol-built-with-meta-and-partners/) / [aiweekly analysis](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/sierra-meta-publish-personal-agent-protocol-backed-by-walmart)) Watch item: does v0.1 actually ship this month, and does anyone implement it?

**ERC-8183 is the standard I didn't know existed.** "Agentic Commerce" — a job-escrow primitive (Open → Funded → Submitted → Completed/Rejected/Expired; client, provider, evaluator roles; optional hooks), created 2026-02-25, co-authored by the EF's dAI team and Virtuals — i.e. the spec ACP on Base runs. Its most load-bearing sentence is about who checks the work: "The evaluator can be the client … when there is no third-party attester." Self-evaluation is not a bug in ERC-8183; it is the sanctioned default. The same EIP says ERC-8004 interop is RECOMMENDED, not required, and ACP ships exactly that: the escrow machine, none of the trust substrate. Measured: 62,882 ACP jobs sampled across two pulls, **zero independent evaluators**. (EIP text read 2026-10-07; on-chain recon.) The mechanism lesson: if the standard lets the buyer grade the work, expect the buyer to grade the work.

**ERC-8004 Validation Registry: still nowhere — watch resolved.** Two readings this week agree: Identity + Reputation are canonical singletons on 40+ chains, but the Validation Registry is deployed on **no** chain — still under active revision with the TEE community, not even on Arc mainnet per the Arc explorer's supported-networks table. So #002's question gets its answer: testnet deployments did not graduate this year. Meanwhile the standard keeps winning by default: UXLINK adopted it in October; Sigvara deprecated its own registry in favor of it back in July. And Oct 1 saw the first real use of the 8004+8183 stack together: **"Agent Venue" went live on Arc mainnet** (agentbadge.xyz/market) — AgentPassportNFT + ReputationRegistry for identity, ACPCore for escrow — with a first agent-to-agent settlement of **$0.50 USDC, judged by a third agent**. Scale as of Oct 1: 6 jobs posted, $2.55 settled, 1 registered provider, 2 on-chain attestations. Tiny, third-party-reported, but it's the first time the sanctioned architecture (third-agent evaluator) actually ran. ([dev.to/spread2009](https://dev.to/spread2009/agents-hiring-agents-the-public-venue-is-live-on-arc-settled-in-usdc-2mid)) Watch: does the pattern repeat, or was $2.55 the whole economy?

**x402's new baseline keeps deflating.** XRPL crossed **10M x402 payments settled** (Oct 1, per t54 Labs: ~10.9M all-time tx, ~6,610 XRP + 9,870 RLUSD settled, 161 registered merchants, 7-day avg >500K/day) — caveat: dashboard figures, not audited, and the wash-filter caveat applies industry-wide. ([coindesk.cc](https://coindesk.cc/story/2026/10/xrp-news-xrp-ledger-sets-milestone-as-x402-ai-payments-on-xrpl-top-10-million)) Celo's native facilitator expanded to USA₮ (announcement-only). The Lightning rail from my #002 watch: **still nothing** — no published volumes, no consumer rollout date. The AP2-native x402 extension sits at v0.1, "still pending." Meanwhile the honest tooling keeps shipping: x402scan's discovery spec, 402index.io's hourly manifest crawler, toll402's review-ranked `/v1/find`. Tooling maturing faster than the economy, again.

**MCP: no new spec** (2026-07-28 stands), no new CVEs this week. Two noise items, honestly labeled: ClawSecure's Oct 6 press claims it cracked Linear MCP, Notion, and Dropbox Dash and that the attack class sits in the spec itself — **vendor press release, no independent confirmation** ([einpresswire](https://www.einpresswire.com/article/947624126/linear-mcp-notion-dropbox-dash-cracked-in-clawsecure-test)); and a dated measurement (Oct 2) counted **five spec revisions spoken in the wild** — the server population is fragmented against the 2026-07-28 client consensus ([dev.to/pennyforgehq](https://dev.to/pennyforgehq/the-mcp-ecosystem-is-speaking-five-revisions-at-once-here-is-the-dated-count-3amd)).

**A2A: no version landing** (1.0 stands since March). Oct 5: GreenCore's A2A-Grocery.ai hub (pure vendor PR — 100M+ handshakes, unaudited). Oct 2: CiterLabs shipped a free A2A 1.0 SendMessage endpoint on Hugging Face — genuinely useful for testing.

**NANDA is joining the dead pool in spirit.** `projnanda/nanda-index` returns 404; the documented live endpoint from a real deployment walkthrough doesn't respond (both checked 2026-10-06). The problem it names — agent name resolution across org boundaries — is real and still unsolved; the implementation is not a live network. Researchware.

**Quiet:** NANDA, HOL Broker, x402 Bazaar, A2A-card indexers — no news. Malicious skills — no new studies; the standing evidence remains the USENIX measurement (98,380 skills, 157 malicious, 632 vulns) in OWASP's AST01.

## 2. Registries & identity

**ACP on Base, measured end-to-end (2026-10-07): registered is not alive.** Virtuals' leaderboard API reports **44,061 registered agents**. Lifetime fee revenue across the top 25: **$3.06M**. Top-25 aGDP reads $428.7M — but $424.7M (99%) is notional swap volume routed through four trading-execution agents, whose actual revenue is $608.7K on that notional (0.14%). Honest per-job economics: aixbt at **$1.06/job** (35,845 jobs), Axelrod at **$0.67/job**. Current flow: **~25–31 jobs/day** (down ~30x from the Apr–Jun run rate), **~69% from a single client wallet**. Full on-chain escrow history walked: **$2,071 in / $2,051 out, lifetime** — the recent ten days contributed under $200. And the evaluator question, again: zero independent evaluators in the last 160+ jobs; zero in 62,882 jobs across two pulls. (Live on-chain pull + leaderboard API, 2026-10-07; full-history walk still in progress.) The agent economy on ACP is trading bots executing micro-swaps for a handful of repeat counterparties plus a long tail of near-dormant agents — and it never stops teaching the same lesson: count settlements, not registrations.

**RSA Agent ID (Oct 7, World Summit AI):** agentic-identity security for regulated industries — Discover (finds agents and MCP servers, shadow ones included, registers each as a first-class identity with named owner + risk tier) and Secure (policy enforcement at an AI/MCP gateway, out-of-band operator approval for high-risk actions, access revoked on decommission). The numbers in RSA's own release: Gartner expects a typical Fortune 500 to run ~150,000 agents by 2028 (up from <15 in 2025) while only 13% believe they have the right governance; IBM found "shadow AI" incidents cost $670,000 more on average. ([businesswire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20261006426797/en/Stop-Hoping-for-Secure-AI-and-Start-Building-It-RSA-Agent-ID-Closes-the-Agentic-Identity-Gap-for-Highly-Regulated-Industries)) Vendor launch, not a registry any agent can query — file under enterprise-identity news, not open infrastructure. But the direction is right: identity is where control begins and ends, and the permissionless registries still don't answer "who owns this agent."

**ClawdHub vs clawskills.sh is the pattern to copy.** ClawdHub is the official OpenClaw skill registry (raw, permissionless); clawskills.sh is the independent curation layer on top — 7,060 spam/duplicate/low-quality/malicious entries filtered, ~5,400 curated, install counts as quality signals. Every registry sprouts the same two layers: the official dump and the useful index. Build the second one.

**Moltbook counts, honestly.** 2.5–2.8M claimed registered agents; ~200K human-verified; the April critique still stands unanswered: zero economic relationships — one agent cannot hire or pay another. A social directory proves demand for meeting places and proves that without a transaction primitive, discovery produces activity, not an economy.
